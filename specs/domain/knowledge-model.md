# Knowledge Model

## Conceptual Framework

This document defines Pekopeko's conceptual knowledge model independently from any implementation technology. The model establishes the fundamental semantic concepts that underlie how information is represented, processed, and managed within the system.

## Core Concepts

### Source
A source represents the original material or context from which information originates. Sources can be documents, conversations, databases, web pages, audio recordings, video content, or other forms of information carriers. A source contains raw data that may need interpretation or extraction to become knowledge.

### Knowledge
Knowledge is information that has been processed, organized, and potentially validated within the Pekopeko system. It represents meaningful information that can be retrieved, reasoned about, and used for decision-making. Knowledge exists in various states and forms throughout its lifecycle.

### Assertion
An assertion is a statement or proposition that claims something to be true or valid. Assertions are typically generated by AI processing of sources or user input, and may represent facts, interpretations, relationships, or inferences. Each assertion has associated metadata describing its origin and status.

### Entity
An entity represents a distinct, identifiable object or concept within the knowledge system. Entities can be people, places, organizations, objects, concepts, or other discrete items that can be meaningfully referenced and related to one another. Entities are identified by unique identifiers regardless of their representation.

### Event
An event represents an occurrence or action that takes place within a specific time frame. Events may be personal activities, historical occurrences, fictional happenings, or any temporally situated action or state change. Events can have temporal properties including start time, end time, duration, and recurrence patterns.

### Relationship
A relationship describes the connection between two or more knowledge elements (entities, events, or other relationships). Relationships capture how different pieces of information relate to each other and may be directional, bidirectional, or contextual. They enable reasoning across interconnected knowledge.

### Proposal
A proposal is a knowledge item that has been generated by the system but has not yet been accepted into canonical status. Proposals are presented to users for review and validation before becoming part of the persistent knowledge base. All knowledge items must pass through the proposal stage before canonical acceptance.

### Canonical Knowledge
Canonical knowledge represents information that has been explicitly accepted by the user as part of the persistent knowledge system. Canonical knowledge is validated, human-reviewed, and forms the authoritative basis for reasoning and derivation within the system. It maintains provenance and historical tracking.

### Derived Knowledge
Derived knowledge is information produced through reasoning, analysis, aggregation, transformation, or inference from other knowledge items. Derived knowledge must be traceable to its supporting inputs and may become stale if its supporting knowledge changes. It represents the value-added processing that transforms raw information into insights.

### Provenance
Provenance refers to the complete history and origin of a knowledge item, including source material, processing steps, validation events, and transformation history. Provenance enables auditability and traceability of important knowledge items throughout their lifecycle.

### Representation
A representation is how knowledge is expressed or displayed in specific interfaces, formats, or systems. Representations may include GUI views, export files, API responses, database records, or other forms of knowledge presentation. A representation is not the canonical knowledge itself.

### Domain
A domain represents a category or classification of knowledge with shared characteristics and context. Domains provide conceptual boundaries for organizing different types of information while maintaining isolation between unrelated knowledge categories. Examples include PERSONAL, FICTION, LEARNING, RESEARCH, PUBLISHING.

### Context / Universe
A context or universe represents a specific instance or setting within a domain where knowledge is applied or interpreted. Contexts are more granular than domains and provide temporal, situational, or thematic specificity. For example, in the FICTION domain, different novels or shared fictional universes would be contexts.

### Validation
Validation is the process by which proposed knowledge items are reviewed and either accepted, rejected, or modified before becoming canonical. The validation process ensures human control over important knowledge decisions and maintains the system's reliability principle.

### Epistemic Status
Epistemic status describes the nature of how knowledge was obtained or derived. This includes whether information is direct (from a source), inferred (by reasoning), uncertain (with confidence limits), or contested (disputed by sources or context).

### Lifecycle Status
Lifecycle status describes where in its evolution a knowledge item currently stands. This includes ACTIVE (current canonical state), SUPERSEDED (replaced by newer information), STALE (potentially outdated), or INVALIDATED (no longer valid).

### Temporal Validity
Temporal validity describes the time period during which a knowledge item is considered valid or applicable. This includes when it was recorded, when it claims to be true, and its effective duration.

### Cross-Domain Task / Operation
A cross-domain task or operation represents a deliberate process that explicitly accesses or reasons across multiple domains. These operations are authorized and distinguishable from default behavior, ensuring domain isolation while enabling integrated analysis.

## Conceptual Relationships

The concepts in this model interrelate as follows:

- Sources provide raw material for knowledge extraction and interpretation
- AI processes sources to generate assertions and proposals
- Proposals undergo validation before becoming canonical knowledge
- Canonical knowledge may be used to derive new knowledge through reasoning
- Knowledge items maintain provenance throughout their lifecycle
- Multiple representations can express the same canonical knowledge
- Domains provide conceptual boundaries while contexts provide specificity
- Cross-domain operations enable integrated analysis while maintaining isolation

## Canonical Conceptual Flow

INPUT
→ SOURCE
→ AI INTERPRETATION / EXTRACTION
→ PROPOSAL
→ HUMAN REVIEW
→ CANONICAL KNOWLEDGE
→ REASONING / DERIVATION
→ DERIVED KNOWLEDGE
→ REPRESENTATIONS / TASK RESULTS

This represents a conceptual lifecycle, not an implementation architecture.

## Open Questions

2. How should the system handle knowledge that spans multiple domains but is contextually unified?
3. What are the appropriate temporal granularity levels for validity tracking?
4. Should there be a distinction between "source truth" and "system interpretation" in addition to the existing concepts?
